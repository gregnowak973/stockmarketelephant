Win rate is a vanity metric

Sep 30, 2026 · 5 min read

Everyone wants a higher win rate. So we bought one, at every price the market offered, and recorded what it cost. Same strategy, same stops — only the profit target changes:

Take profit atWin rateTotal profit
0.5× risk66%$4,543
1× risk55%$14,096
1.5× risk52%$14,557
2× risk51%$12,910
3× risk / ride to close51%$14,803

The 66%-win version makes less than a third of the money. Every extra point of win rate is purchased by selling a winner early: you keep all the same losers and convert the +2R and +3R days into +0.5R days. The equity curve gets smoother wins and the same rough losses.

Win rate tells you how often you're right. It says nothing about whether being right pays. A 35%-win trend system and a 90%-win option-selling system can both be excellent — and both can be ruinous.

The honest structure of our table: everything from 1× to 3× is statistically indistinguishable (we ran the formal test; the differences are sampling noise), and below 1× is a cliff. So the target barely matters — which itself is informative: this strategy's engine is holding to the close, and the target is decoration.

One real use for the win-rate dial: psychology. If you genuinely cannot sit through a 51%-win system's losing streaks — and at 4 trades/week, losing 5 of 7 happens regularly by pure chance — running 1× costs approximately nothing and wins 55%. Just know you're buying comfort, not edge, and decide before you start, not mid-drawdown.
